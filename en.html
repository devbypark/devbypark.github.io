01 / FDE FIT

What an FDE does,
and what I've done.

My title is Solution Engineer.
I did all of the work below on customer projects.

  1. 01Turn customer needs into technical targets

    For an API-only customer, what mattered was not throughput but the response time of a single request. I set that as the optimization target and, with our AI engineer, cut time per request from 20–30 seconds to about 2–3 seconds after warm-up.

  2. 02Run a benchmark under unfamiliar constraints

    Because the evaluation documents could not be known in advance, I first built an evaluation flow that could review even unseen documents right away. On site, I did the labeling, ground truth creation, and prompt revisions myself to run the benchmark.

  3. 03Build from frontend to model serving

    I solely developed the frontend and backend that connect document intake, extraction, review, masking, and external integration into one product flow, and contributed to model and serving work.

  4. 04Decide with evals and instrumentation

    I made decisions against ground truth and per-stage timing. Pre-validation on 200 pages showed zero failures and 97.2% weighted recall (IoU 0.5), and I did not adopt settings that improved speed but fell short on detection accuracy.

  5. 05Integrate with customer systems and timelines

    So the customer's RPA development would not wait on extraction, I delivered the API field specification and input validation first and handed over new field extraction afterward.At a previous company, I agreed payment request, callback, and webhook specs directly with partner developers and built the integration APIs.

  6. 06Install in closed networks and own what happens after launch

    All 6 projects ran in closed networks, so I installed everything from drivers to model files as offline bundles. When failures occurred after delivery, I found the root cause myself, restored service, and built the fix into the next deployment package so it would not recur.

  7. 07Make LLM output trustworthy

    When the model server read a schema's value patterns differently and Korean names came out romanized, I fixed it by checking patterns against the model server's rules at save time. When a latency setting caused degenerate model output, the 681-document evaluation set caught it, and I confirmed the cause and reverted it.

I believe the experience of defining customer goals, setting evaluation criteria, optimizing inference, integrating with customer systems, and operating document AI can carry over to AI products in many other domains.

04 / HOW I ENGAGE

Context before technology.

Four stages that take a customer project to the end.
Each one comes from a real case.

  1. 01

    Set the target from how the customer works

    For an API-only customer, response time came before throughput. For a customer storing documents on NAS, the intake path came first. Where RPA timelines were at stake, delivery timing came first.

    SCOPE
  2. 02

    Prove it first with ground truth and timing data

    I check errors and bottlenecks against ground truth and per-stage timing, then compare quality and speed together to choose processing code, serving settings, and prompts.

    PROVE
  3. 03

    Make it installable in the customer's environment

    I organize deployment configuration for the application, workers, and GPU services, along with DB migration, permission and integrity checks, and preflight procedures, then deploy on site.

    SHIP
  4. 04

    Feed what operations reveals back into the product

    I keep maintaining systems after delivery and turn customer meetings and field feedback into product improvements such as schemas, review screens, and intake paths.

    OPERATE

How a system deploys and operates in the real world matters as much as how the model behaves.
